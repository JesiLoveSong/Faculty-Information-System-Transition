# Faculty-Information-System-Transition

**Project Overview**
---

This project evaluated faculty, staff, and administrator perceptions of the university's Faculty Information System (FIS) to identify user needs, assess satisfaction with the existing platform, and inform decisions regarding a potential system transition. I designed and administered a campus-wide survey, analyzed quantitative and qualitative feedback, and presented findings to project stakeholders and senior leadership to support vendor evaluation, implementation planning, and long-term system strategy.

**Question**
---

What insights can be gathered from faculty and other stakeholders regarding their experiences with the current Faculty Information System, and how do those perceptions inform decisions about adopting a new platform?

**Project Objectives**
---

- **Serve as project lead** for Faculty Information System initiative.
- Assess stakeholder satisfaction with the existing FIS.
- Identify common usability, functionality, and workflow challenges experienced by users.
- Understand stakeholder priorities regarding system capabilities and future improvements.
- Provide leadership with evidence-based recommendations to support system evaluation and procurement decisions.
- Inform implementation planning and change management strategies should a system transition occur.

**Methodology**
---

**Survey Design & Administration**

- Used peer-reviewed survey instruments to develop a university-wide survey using Qualtrics.
- Designed survey questions to capture stakeholder experiences, system usage patterns, challenges, and preferences regarding future system options.
- Distributed the survey to faculty, staff, and administrative stakeholders.
- Collected both quantitative responses and qualitative feedback through open-ended survey questions.

**Data Analysis**

- Analyzed survey response frequencies and stakeholder preferences.
- Categorized open-ended responses into major themes and sentiment categories.
- Compared concerns and priorities across stakeholder groups.
- Evaluated patterns related to system satisfaction, change readiness, and perceived organizational needs.

**Reporting and Visualization**

- Summarized survey findings using Excel-based analyses and visualizations.
- Developed executive-level reports highlighting stakeholder sentiment, key concerns, and decision-making considerations.
- Presented findings to project team and senior leadership.

**Key Findings**
---

- Among respondents with a clear preference, 74% favored switching systems, compared with 26% who preferred remaining with the existing platform.
- Usability and interface challenges were the most frequently reported concerns, accounting for 34% of comments. Common issues included excessive clicks, difficulty locating information, confusing navigation, and challenges retrieving documents.
- Implementation and institutional process concerns as another significant theme, representing 21% of comments, followed by change fatigue (18%) and cost/value considerations (12%).
- Stakeholder priorities differed by role. Faculty prioritized usability and reducing administrative burden, staff emphasized workflow automation, and administrators focused primarily on implementation and operational efficiency.
- The analysis helped distinguish between platform-related limitations and broader institutional implementation challenges.
- The insights informed institutional investment decisions, vendor evaluations, implementation planning, and change management strategies.
- Per the results, the institution chose to switch to a new FIS.

**Business Recommendations**
---

- Future system evaluations should place significant emphasis on usability, intuitive navigation, and overall user experience, as these issues represented the most common source of stakeholder dissatisfaction.
- Address concerns related to change fatigue through proactive communication, stakeholder engagement, and phased implementation planning to support adoption and reduce resistance.
- Develop role-specific training resources and ongoing support structures to increase user confidence and maximize system adoption following implementation
- Establish ongoing feedback mechanisms to evaluate user experience, identify improvement opportunities, and support continuous optimization of system processes.

**Analytical Limitations**
---

- Survey findings reflected stakeholder perceptions and experiences rather than direct measures of system performance.
- The survey received approximately 110 responses from a potential population of more than 2,000 faculty members, in addition to university staff, resulting in a relatively small response rate. While the findings provided valuable insight into stakeholder perspectives, the results may not fully represent the views and experiences of the broader university community.
- While thematic analysis identified common concerns and priorities, qualitative feedback cannot fully capture the complexity and diversity of stakeholder experiences across all colleges and administrative units.
  
**Conclusion**
---

The survey analysis provided valuable insight into stakeholder experiences with the university's Faculty Information System and identified key factors influencing perceptions of a potential system transition. Findings revealed broad support for adopting a new platform, with usability concerns emerging as the most significant driver of dissatisfaction among users.

The findings informed vendor evaluation, implementation planning, and change management strategies, ultimately contributing to the institution's decision to transition to a new Faculty Information System. This project demonstrates how survey research and stakeholder analysis can support major institutional technology decisions through evidence-based decision-making.
